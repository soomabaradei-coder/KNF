Preparing AI talent for Saudi Arabia's future

The proposed Bachelor of Science in Artificial Intelligence at King Abdulaziz University's Faculty of Computing and Information Technology (FCIT) is designed to contribute to the ambitions of Saudi Vision 2030 and the National Strategy for Data and AI. It offers a dedicated pathway for preparing graduates with the technical expertise, practical experience, and understanding of responsible AI needed to support the Kingdom's digital transformation.

Building on KAU's established computing foundation, the proposed curriculum includes 13 dedicated AI courses. Its design draws on benchmarking against selected programs at Carnegie Mellon University, MIT, the National University of Singapore, and Alfaisal University. These comparisons inform the program's academic depth and progression, while its emphasis on Arabic-language applications and local challenges reflects the Saudi context. The curriculum is also designed with relevant national and international accreditation requirements in view.

Students will progress from computing and mathematical foundations to designing, evaluating, and deploying AI systems. Responsible AI, Arabic-language capabilities, mandatory field training, and a deployment-focused capstone project connect academic learning with practical application.

The program aims to prepare graduates who can translate AI methods into useful, responsible products and systems, equipped to contribute to Saudi industry, public-sector innovation, and research.

What You Will Learn

Five questions the degree answers, each with named required courses

How AI learns from data

Classical learning in AI 332, deep learning in AI 336, and a layer few programs require: engineering the data itself in AI 342, because trustworthy models begin with disciplined data.

How AI understands the world

Language and vision are both compulsory, in AI 338 Multimodal Machine Learning, and at the same depth: of its forty-five lecture hours, sixteen are language, sixteen are vision, and thirteen are the transformer and the multimodal alignment the two share. The language block carries a dedicated unit on Arabic morphology, orthography and dialects that no foreign programme carries. SDAIA names computer vision and natural language processing as two separate competencies of the AI Engineer, and this course carries both rather than treating one as an appendix to the other. Physical sensing sits in AI 334. And it understands Arabic: a mandatory unit no foreign program carries.

How AI decides and acts

Search, planning and reasoning in AI 331; action in the physical world in AI 334; and autonomous agents in AI 335, where deciding and acting become literal.

How AI is used responsibly

A full early course, AI 339, paired with the first AI course, grounded in SDAIA principles and the Saudi data-protection law, not in borrowed case studies.

How AI becomes a product that reaches the market, and serves Vision 2030

Where other narratives stop, ours continues: systems (AI 360), product engineering and MLOps (AI 350), mandatory field training, and a two-semester capstone ending in a deployed system defended before a panel, the model-to-market capability the National Strategy for Data and AI calls for.

Where this plan came from

The programme was first designed at 150 credit hours over ten semesters, on the template of the new Computer Science plan. Open the earlier 150 plan

The 150-credit design mirrored the Computer Science plan exactly: 23 credit hours of university requirements, 42 of college requirements, 67 of compulsory major, 12 of electives and 6 free, over ten semesters, with students allocated to the major after a three-semester shared trunk. It is retained here for the record because the council approved the programme concept on it, and because every argument about depth and coverage was first settled against it.

Direct admission, and the trunk opens up

Students now enter the programme in semester one instead of being allocated after the shared college trunk. Introduction to Computing, Emerging Technologies and Computing Issues and Ethics leave the plan; the general ethics foundation opens AI 339.

Institution requirements concentrated

One Islamic culture course and one Arabic course, and the English courses are replaced by an English admission requirement, since the programme is taught in English from English references.

Mergers that removed repetition, not content

Object-oriented programming merged into AI 203 at five credit hours; data structures and algorithms into AI 204 on the MIT and NUS model; computing ethics into AI 339; operating systems, networks and security into AI 360 at five credit hours.

What the national standard gained

The 150 plan carried four credit hours of physics and no chemistry, and relied on the flexibility clause. This plan carries seven and four, so the ETEC enabling units close at exactly their thirty-credit minimum rather than by exception.

The 150-credit plan of record is withdrawn by decision of the programme committee. This page describes the plan in force.

Aim a Role at a Domain

Rows of choice, not a maze: the student completes a role track (three designated courses), then aims it at a domain through the fourth elective, the training placement and the capstone. Pick any pair to see the result.

How the tracks sit on the national occupations. The SDAIA National Occupational Standard Framework for Data and Artificial Intelligence sorts the field into five classifications, ten specialty areas and sixteen occupations, and in its Annex A it defines five core career paths, each in five grades from Engineer to Chief: Data Engineer, Data Scientist, Data Analyst, AI Engineer and AI Researcher. The AI Engineer path begins at fresh graduate with a bachelor degree, and the framework lists its required competencies by name: computer vision, natural language processing, deep learning, data science, DevOps, agile development, privacy and security compliance, data engineering and data governance. Every one of those nine is a required course in this plan, which is why the role tracks are depth inside a national occupation rather than a parallel invention. The framework also names the certifications we pair with the tracks, and it lists a readiness certificate from the Education and Training Evaluation Commission among the accreditation requirements of the entry grade.

Every graduate, whatever the pathway, leaves with the same four proofs: a deployed capstone defended before a panel, eight weeks of mandatory field training, a pathway certification or partner credential, and Arabic-capable AI skills no foreign program carries.

A CLOSING NOTE

This is a high-performance design, and it lands in a college that already holds its enablers. FCIT runs the established Computer Science program whose new plan this one mirrors semester for semester; its faculty already teach the mathematics, programming and systems layers of this very trunk; its laboratories, quality system and governance councils are in daily operation; and its training pathways already place students in the field every year.

So this plan does not ask the college to build a runway. It asks it to schedule the flight: assign the AI course staffing, equip the GPU laboratory, and sign the training agreements. A fair external reviewer would write: “an exceptionally mature design, presented by a college that already owns most of what the design needs.”

With full appreciation for the role of Computer Science in founding and teaching artificial intelligence, meeting the compulsory requirements of both disciplines at the depth each one demands calls for sufficient room for each. That is where an independent artificial intelligence program earns its place: it meets its own specialty standards and learning outcomes, and it strengthens integration with Computer Science.
